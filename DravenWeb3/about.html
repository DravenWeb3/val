01 — HOW I KNOW WHAT I KNOW

Contact Epistemology

I don't accept a claim because an institution stands behind it, and I don't reject one because it's popular. I test it against structure — strip the story off, check whether the mechanism still holds. If an argument needs its packaging to survive, it isn't an argument.

My hardest standard: I only keep something as knowledge once it has a physical consequence I can reach with my own body. Everything I read — including anything an AI tells me — is an activation key for something I already know first-hand, not a replacement for knowing it. I use AI to move faster, never to think for me.

02 — WHAT I'M ACTUALLY BUILDING TOWARD

The legitimacy transition

The move from centralized to decentralized finance isn't a technology upgrade wearing a bigger story. It's a legitimacy event. Every institution built on an information gap loses its authority the instant that gap becomes something anyone can check for themselves — not gradually, the instant.

The actual fight: institutions that say "trust us, you can't check" against systems that say "verify it yourself, right now, for free." I'm building the second kind of system, one function at a time, and publishing the build in the open so the thesis has to survive contact with reality — not just with an argument.

04 — HOW I WORK

Rules I don't break for convenience

Value has to be real, and it has to stay reachable. Value that's fake, and value that's real but seizable by someone who isn't its owner — both treated as defects, not tradeoffs.

Verification over trust, including my own claims. Nothing here gets a pass because I said it. It gets checked against what actually shipped.

Portable by default. Core data stays in formats I own — plain text, Postgres. I rent tools when the output stays mine; I never let critical logic get welded to someone else's API.

Signal over engagement. I don't optimize for retention. I optimize for how much is actually true and useful per minute you spend on it.
